2.5.2 Combustors and Burners

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.5 • LO 2.1, 2.2

Learning focus: Distinguish premixed and diffusion burning, and name the design variables that control burner and combustor performance.

Lecture notes

TypeHow fuel and air meetExamplesWhat controls the burn rate
Premixedmixed before ignition; a flame front travels through the chargeSI engine, Bunsen burner with air openmixture strength \(\phi\) (flame speed peaks slightly rich), turbulence
Diffusion (non-premixed)fuel and air meet at the flame surfaceCI engine spray burn, boiler burner, candlemixing rate of fuel and air

A diesel engine shows both: the fuel that evaporates during the ignition delay burns rapidly as a premixed charge, and the remaining fuel burns as a diffusion flame as it mixes. This is the reason the Dual cycle (page 1.2.4) uses a constant-volume and a constant-pressure heat-addition step.

Design variables

  • Air supply: too little air gives CO and soot, too much air carries heat out of the stack.
  • Mixing: swirl, injection pressure and burner geometry set the burn rate.
  • Residence time: the gases must stay hot long enough to burn out.
  • Flame stability: the flame must stay anchored without blow-off or flashback.

Note:

Page 1.2.4 explained the Dual cycle as a model of premixed plus diffusion combustion in CI engines. Here you see the physics behind its two heat-addition steps.
