2.6.1 Engine Heat and Mass Balance

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.6 • LO 2.2, 2.3

Learning focus: Perform a complete mass and energy balance on an engine from test data and present it as a heat balance table.

Lecture notes

Two bookkeeping statements govern every device: mass in equals mass out, and energy in equals useful output plus losses. If the balance does not close, a measurement is wrong or a loss was forgotten, so the balance is also the quality check of an engine test.

Mass balance

\[\dot{m}_{\mathrm{exh}}=\dot{m}_a+\dot{m}_f\]

Source: Heywood, Ch. 12 (energy and mass accounting); Pulkrabek, Ch. 10; Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters.

Energy balance of the engine

\[\dot{m}_f\,\mathrm{LHV}=\dot{W}_b+\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{cool}}+\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{exh}}+\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{oth}}\]

\(\dot Q_{oth}\) collects friction to oil, radiation, unburned fuel and unaccounted losses (found by difference).

Source: Heywood, Ch. 12; Pulkrabek, Ch. 10 (energy distribution); Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters.

Heat to coolant and to exhaust

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{cool}}=\dot{m}_w\,c_w\,\Delta T_w\]

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{exh}}=\dot{m}_{\mathrm{exh}}\,c_p\left(T_{\mathrm{exh}}-T_0\right)\]

\(\dot m_w, c_w, \Delta T_w\) coolant flow, specific heat and temperature rise; \(T_0\) is the ambient reference temperature.

Source: Heywood, Ch. 12; Pulkrabek, Ch. 10; Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters).

Worked Example 9 — Engine heat balance

Test data: \(P_b=40\) kW; fuel 0.0028 kg/s with LHV 44,000 kJ/kg; air 0.042 kg/s; coolant 0.45 kg/s with a 15 K rise (\(c_w=4.18\)); exhaust at 600 °C, ambient 25 °C, \(c_p=1.15\) kJ/kg·K.

\[\dot{m}_{\mathrm{exh}}=0.042+0.0028=0.0448\ \mathrm{kg/s}\]

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{in}}=0.0028\,(44000)=123.2\ \mathrm{kW}\]

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{cool}}=0.45\,(4.18)(15)=28.2\ \mathrm{kW}\]

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{exh}}=0.0448\,(1.15)(600-25)=29.6\ \mathrm{kW}\]

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{oth}}=123.2-40-28.2-29.6=25.4\ \mathrm{kW}\]

Answer: Brake 40 kW (32.5%); coolant 28.2 kW (22.9%); exhaust 29.6 kW (24.0%); other 25.4 kW (20.6%). Also \(\phi=14.7/15=0.98\).

A typical SI engine puts roughly one third of fuel energy into brake work, one third into coolant and one third into exhaust; this test is consistent with that pattern.

Method from: Heywood, Ch. 12; Pulkrabek, Ch. 10.

Note:

Brake work and indicated work from Unit I (page 1.1.1: indicated work, brake work, friction work and mechanical efficiency) split the shaft work from the friction portion that appears here in \(\dot Q_{oth}\). The brake thermal efficiency from this balance is \(\eta_b=\dot W_b/(\dot m_f\,\mathrm{LHV})\), the same quantity as in Problem Set 1, Problem 6.

Class Activity — Pair-solving on energy balance

Change the values from Example 9 to \(P_b=45\) kW and exhaust at 650 °C. Rebuild the heat balance table, then sketch the Sankey diagram. Predict how the table changes if the mixture is leaned to \(\phi=0.85\).
