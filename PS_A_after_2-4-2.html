PROBLEM SET QUESTIONS

Problem 1: Gas Fuel Burned with 25% Excess Air

(Reference: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4; Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion chapters))

A natural-gas blend has the volumetric (mole) analysis CH\(_4\) = 75.5% and C\(_2\)H\(_6\) = 24.5%. It burns completely with 25% excess air supplied at 100 kPa and 25 °C.

Calculate:

  • The balanced reaction per kmol of fuel.
  • The actual air-fuel ratio, in kg air/kg fuel.
  • The volumetric analysis of the dry flue gas, and the gas constant \(R\) of the dry flue gas, in kJ/kg·K.
  • The mass fractions of carbon and hydrogen in the fuel, and the theoretical air-fuel ratio from \((A/F)_{theo}=11.5\,C+34.5\,(H-O/8)+4.3\,S\). Compare with your mole-balance result.
  • The dew-point temperature of the products. Take the total pressure as 100 kPa.

Problem 2: Octane: Excess Air from a Dry Product Analysis with CO

(Reference: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion chapters))

Octane (C\(_8\)H\(_{18}\)) is burned with dry air. The volumetric analysis of the products on a dry basis is CO\(_2\) = 10.02%, CO = 0.88%, O\(_2\) = 5.62%, N\(_2\) = 83.48%.

Calculate:

  • The balanced reaction per kmol of fuel (find the water formed from a hydrogen balance).
  • The actual air-fuel ratio.
  • The percent excess air from an atom balance.
  • The percent excess air from the dry-analysis formula \(e=\dfrac{y_{O_2}-0.5\,y_{CO}}{0.264\,y_{N_2}-(y_{O_2}-0.5\,y_{CO})}\), and the difference from part 3 with a reason.
  • The dew-point temperature of the products at 101.325 kPa.
