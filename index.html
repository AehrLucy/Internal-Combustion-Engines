ME 3115; CO2 &bull

Learning focus: Start here. This page explains what Unit II covers, how it builds on Unit I, and the order of the pages that follow.

Why Unit II?

Unit I told you how much work an engine can extract from a given heat input. Unit II tells you where that heat comes from. You will learn to write the reaction for a fuel, find the air it needs, judge whether the mixture is rich or lean, and account for every kilogram and kilojoule through a burner, boiler or engine cylinder.

Course outcome and intended learning outcomes

CO2. Solve complex thermochemical and performance parameters of fuels and engine operations by calculating equivalence ratios, air-fuel mixtures, mass balances, and indicator diagrams within standard engineering accuracy limits.

  • 2.1 Calculate thermochemical properties, equivalence ratios, combustion product mixtures, and fuel processing metrics for solid, liquid, and gaseous fuels.
  • 2.2 Perform complete heat and mass balance calculations across combustors, burners, boilers, and engine cylinders.
  • 2.3 Formulate combustion rate and energy release equations to evaluate thermal efficiency losses due to incomplete combustion.

Assessment in this unit (from the syllabus)

  • Quiz 2 and 3: Fuel Thermochemistry and Gas Mixtures, graded with step-by-step analytical rubrics.
  • Modular Problem Sets: thermochemical mass and heat balance solutions.
  • Activities: hand-calculation seatworks,problem-solving on equivalence ratios and exhaust gas analysis, asynchronous combustion balance worksheets, and in-class solving on air-fuel ratio and energy balance.
