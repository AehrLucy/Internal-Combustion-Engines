Problem 3: Furnace Burning Natural Gas with a Forced-Draft Fan

(Reference: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4; Class notes (no textbook cited; instructor to confirm a reference))

A furnace burns natural gas with volumetric analysis CH\(_4\) = 85%, C\(_2\)H\(_6\) = 12% and C\(_3\)H\(_8\) = 3%. The gas flows at 0.37 m\(^3\)/s (measured at 25 °C and 101.325 kPa), and 25% excess air is needed for complete combustion. The combustion air at 25 °C and 1 atm is supplied by a forced-draft fan through a short duct of 0.25 m\(^2\) cross-section, against a static pressure of 50 mm H\(_2\)O. The fan efficiency is 65%. Treat the products as an ideal gas at 25 °C and 101.325 kPa, including their water vapor.

Calculate:

  • The actual air-fuel ratio, in kg air/kg fuel.
  • The volume flow rate of the products of combustion, in m\(^3\)/s.
  • The power output of the forced-draft fan motor, in kW.
  • The mass percentages of hydrogen and carbon in the fuel.
