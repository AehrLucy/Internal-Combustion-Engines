2.5.3 Combustion Rate and Energy Release

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.5 • LO 2.3

Learning focus: Formulate the heat release rate and combustion efficiency, and evaluate the efficiency loss from incomplete combustion.

Lecture notes

Not all the fuel energy is released. When a mixture is rich, or burns incompletely, CO, H₂ and unburned hydrocarbons leave in the exhaust still carrying chemical energy. The combustion efficiency \(\eta_c\) measures the fraction of fuel energy that was released.

Heat release rate

\[\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\eta_c\,\dot{m}_f\,\mathrm{LHV}\]

Source: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3 and Heywood, Ch. 9 (heat release analysis); Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4.

Combustion efficiency from unburned species

\[\eta_c=1-\frac{\sum_i m_i\,\mathrm{LHV}_i}{m_f\,\mathrm{LHV}}\]

\(m_i\) is the mass of each unburned species (CO, H₂, HC) with heating value \(\mathrm{LHV}_i\). For CO, about 283,000 kJ/kmol is lost per kmol that is not oxidized to CO₂.

Source: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Ferguson & Kirkpatrick, Internal Combustion Engines: Applied Thermosciences, Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4.

Burn fraction versus crank angle (Wiebe function)

\[x_b=1-\exp\left[-a\left(\frac{\theta-\theta_0}{\Delta\theta}\right)^{m+1}\right]\]

\(\theta_0\) start of combustion, \(\Delta\theta\) burn duration; typical \(a\approx5\), \(m\approx2\) for SI engines, fitted to measured heat release.

Source: Heywood, Ch. 9 (combustion modeling and heat-release analysis).

Worked Example 8 — Efficiency loss from CO

One kmol of C₈H₁₈ (LHV 44,400 kJ/kg) burns rich and the products contain 3 kmol of CO instead of CO₂. Estimate \(\eta_c\).

\[Q_{\mathrm{in}}=114.23\,(44400)=5.07\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{kJ}\]

\[Q_{\mathrm{unreleased}}=3\,(283000)=849000\ \mathrm{kJ}\]

\[\eta_c=1-\frac{849000}{5.07\times10^{6}}=0.833\]

Answer: \(\eta_c\approx0.833\), a loss of 16.7% of the fuel energy.

Unburned H₂ and hydrocarbons would reduce it further. Incomplete combustion is a double penalty: lost energy and CO emission.

Method from: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4.

Note:

On the indicator diagram of page 1.3.1 and the Otto cycle, heat is added at constant volume at TDC. The Wiebe curve shows how real combustion departs from this: a faster, earlier burn moves the real cycle toward the ideal Otto cycle, while a slow burn lowers peak pressure and efficiency.
