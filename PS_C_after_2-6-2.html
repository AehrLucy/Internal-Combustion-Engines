Problem 4: Diesel Power Plant: Day Tank, Cooling Water and Exhaust Loss

(Reference: Heywood, Ch. 12; Class notes (no textbook cited; instructor to confirm a reference))

A commercial complex operates a diesel power plant for its 24-hour electric needs. The plant has one supercharged 6-cylinder, 800 Bhp, 800 rpm, 4-stroke diesel engine. At full load the brake specific fuel consumption is 0.175 kg/Bhp·h. The fuel is at 28 °C with a density based on 26 °API. The day tank is a closed cylinder with diameter equal to height, and it is full when it holds one day of fuel. Cooling-water temperature rise through the jacket is 15 °C and the cooling loss is 30% of the heat supplied by the fuel. Friction and other unaccounted losses are 8%.

Calculate:

  • The specific gravity of the fuel at 28 °C and its density, in kg/m\(^3\).
  • The most economical size of the day tank (diameter = height), in m.
  • The mass flow rate of cooling water, in kg/s.
  • The percentage of fuel heat leaving as brake power, and the heat lost in the exhaust gases, in % and kW.

Problem 5: Waste-Heat Recovery Boiler on a Diesel Engine

(Reference: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Heywood, Ch. 12; Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion chapters))

A waste-heat recovery boiler is being considered to recover 35% of the heat in the exhaust gas of a 1600 kW (brake) diesel engine burning C\(_{12}\)H\(_{26}\). Brake specific fuel consumption is 0.23 kg/kW·h and excess air is 30%. Air enters at 1 atm and 30 °C and the fuel at 105 kPa and 28 °C. Exhaust gas leaves at 400 °C with specific heat 0.255 kcal/kg·°C. The engine jacket water leaves at 65 °C and is used as boiler feedwater. The boiler efficiency is 65% and it produces dry saturated steam at 250 kPa (absolute; \(h_g=2716.9\) kJ/kg, and \(h_f\) at 65 °C = 272.06 kJ/kg).
Given relation for the heat carried by the moisture formed from the fuel hydrogen (from class notes, source to be confirmed; temperatures in °F inside the bracket, result in kJ/s):

\[Q_{3b}=\dot m_v\,[\,1066+0.5\,t_g-t_F\,]\,(2.326275)\qquad(t_g>575\,^\circ F)\]

Calculate:

  • The actual air-fuel ratio.
  • The volumetric analysis of the dry flue gas.
  • The heat in the exhaust gases, \(Q_3=Q_{3a}+Q_{3b}\), where \(Q_{3a}=\dot m_{dg}\,c_{pg}\,(t_g-t_a)\) for the dry gases and \(\dot m_v=9\,H_2\,\dot m_F\) is the moisture formed.
  • The mass of steam produced, in kg/s and kg/min.
