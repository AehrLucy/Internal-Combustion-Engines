2.2.2 Solid and Gaseous Fuel Calculations

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.2 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Estimate heating value and theoretical air for a solid fuel from its ultimate analysis, and the heating value of a gas mixture from its volumetric analysis.

Solid fuels from ultimate analysis

Let \(C, H, O, S\) be the mass fractions of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sulfur in the as-received fuel and \(M\) the moisture fraction. The Dulong formula estimates the heating value from composition. The term \(H-O/8\) is the “available hydrogen” because part of the hydrogen is already bound to the oxygen in the fuel.

Dulong formula for HHV and LHV of a solid fuel (kJ/kg)

\[\mathrm{HHV}=33950\,C+144200\left(H-\frac{O}{8}\right)+9400\,S\]

\[\mathrm{LHV}=\mathrm{HHV}-2442\left(9H+M\right)\]

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters) (fuel analysis and heating value); Maleev, Internal Combustion Engines: Theory and Design (fuels and combustion chapters).

Theoretical air for a solid fuel (kg air per kg fuel)

\[\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_s=11.5\,C+34.5\left(H-\frac{O}{8}\right)+4.3\,S\]

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters); Maleev, Internal Combustion Engines: Theory and Design (fuels and combustion chapters).

These are estimates. A bomb calorimeter measures the true heating value.

Worked Example 3 — Coal heating value and air requirement

Coal by mass: C 0.70, H 0.05, O 0.08, S 0.02, N 0.01, ash 0.10, moisture 0.04. Find HHV, LHV and theoretical air.

\[\mathrm{HHV}=33950(0.70)+144200\left(0.05-\frac{0.08}{8}\right)+9400(0.02)\]

\[\mathrm{HHV}=23765+5768+188=29721\ \mathrm{kJ/kg}\]

\[\mathrm{LHV}=29721-2442\,(9\times0.05+0.04)=28524\ \mathrm{kJ/kg}\]

\[\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_s=11.5(0.70)+34.5(0.04)+4.3(0.02)=9.52\]

Answer: HHV = 29,721 kJ/kg; LHV = 28,524 kJ/kg; \((A/F)_s=9.52\).

Method from: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters).

Gaseous fuels from volumetric analysis

For ideal gases, volume fraction equals mole fraction \(y_i\). Only the combustible components contribute to the heating value; inert components such as CO₂ and N₂ contribute zero.

Heating value of a gas mixture on a volume basis

\[\mathrm{LHV}_{\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_i y_i\,\mathrm{LHV}_i\]

\(y_i\) is the mole (volume) fraction and \(\mathrm{LHV}_i\) the volumetric LHV of component \(i\) at the same reference conditions.

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters) (gas mixtures); Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4; Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters.

Worked Example 4 — Biogas heating value

Biogas by volume: 60% CH₄, 35% CO₂, 5% N₂. LHV of CH₄ is 35.8 MJ/m³.

\[\mathrm{LHV}_{\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_i y_i\,\mathrm{LHV}_i\]

\[\mathrm{LHV}_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.60\,(35.8)+0.35\,(0)+0.05\,(0)\]

\[\mathrm{LHV}_{\mathrm{mix}}=21.5\ \mathrm{MJ/m^3}\]

Answer: \(\mathrm{LHV}_{mix}=21.5\) MJ/m³.

The inerts also lower flame speed, which matters for burner and engine ignition.

Method from: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters).

Class Activity — Group problem-solving

Each group gets a different solid fuel analysis (wood, lignite, anthracite). Compute HHV, LHV and \((A/F)_s\) with the Dulong relations and rank the fuels by energy per kg of air required.

Check Your Understanding

  1. Why is the term \(H-O/8\) used instead of \(H\)?
  2. What assumptions are hidden in the Dulong formula?
  3. If the CO₂ share in a biogas rises from 35% to 45%, what happens to its LHV?
