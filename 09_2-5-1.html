2.5.1 Equivalence Ratio

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.5 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Compute the equivalence ratio from flow rates and use it to state the mixture strength and the heat input per kg of air.

Lecture notes

Different fuels have different stoichiometric ratios, so \((A/F)\) alone does not tell you how rich or lean a mixture is. Normalizing by the stoichiometric ratio gives a fuel-independent measure of mixture strength, the equivalence ratio \(\phi\).

Equivalence ratio

\[\phi=\frac{(F/A)_{\mathrm{actual}}}{(F/A)_s}=\frac{(A/F)_s}{(A/F)_{\mathrm{actual}}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\]

\(\phi<1\) is lean, \(\phi=1\) stoichiometric, \(\phi>1\) rich.

Source: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3 (definition of equivalence ratio); Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4; Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters.

Converting from excess air. If a problem or your older notes give excess air \(e\) as a fraction, then \(\lambda=1+e\) and \(\phi=1/(1+e)\). For example 25% excess air gives \(\lambda=1.25\) and \(\phi=0.80\) (lean). A rich mixture has \(e<0\): air deficiency of 10% gives \(\lambda=0.90\) and \(\phi=1.11\).

Operation\(\phi\) (approx.)Reason
SI, maximum power1.05 – 1.1slightly rich, fast flame
SI, economyabout 0.9lean, more complete burn

CI, overall0.3 – 0.8load set by fuel quantity

Linking air flow to \(\phi\)

Air flow comes from the volumetric efficiency of Unit I, so \(\phi\) can be found from engine speed and measured fuel flow.

Air mass flow rate of a four-stroke engine

\[\dot{m}_a=e_v\,\rho_i\,V_{d,\mathrm{total}}\,\frac{N}{2}\]

Same quantities as the Unit I Formula Reference: \(e_v\) volumetric efficiency, \(\rho_i\) intake air density, \(V_{d,total}\) total displacement, \(N\) speed (rev/s).

Source: Unit I Formula Reference page; Heywood, Ch. 2; Pulkrabek, Ch. 2.

Heat input per kg of air, from \(\phi\)

\[q_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{\eta_c\,\phi\,\mathrm{LHV}}{(A/F)_s}\]

Obtained from \(q_{in}=\eta_c\,(F/A)\,\mathrm{LHV}\) with \(F/A=\phi/(A/F)_s\).

Source: Derived from the definitions above; compare Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3 and Ferguson & Kirkpatrick, Internal Combustion Engines: Applied Thermosciences, Ch. 3.

Worked Example 7 — Equivalence ratio from flow rates

A gasoline engine with \((A/F)_s=14.7\) takes air at 0.0120 kg/s and fuel at 0.0010 kg/s. Find \(\phi\) and \(\lambda\), then the fuel flow needed at \(\phi=0.85\) for the same air.

\[\frac{A}{F}=\frac{0.0120}{0.0010}=12\]

\[\phi=\frac{14.7}{12}=1.225,\qquad\lambda=\frac{1}{\phi}=0.816\]

\[\dot{m}_f=\frac{\dot{m}_a}{(A/F)_s/\phi}=\frac{0.0120}{14.7/0.85}=0.000694\ \mathrm{kg/s}\]

Answer: \(\phi=1.225\) (rich), \(\lambda=0.816\); fuel flow 0.000694 kg/s at \(\phi=0.85\).

Method from: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4.

Note!

Problem Set 1, Problem 4 gives the Dual cycle a total heat input of 1400 kJ/kg of air. What equivalence ratio would burn that much fuel? With a gasoline-type fuel, LHV = 44,000 kJ/kg, \((A/F)_s=14.7\) and \(\eta_c=1\), rearrange the heat-input formula: \(\phi=q_{in}(A/F)_s/(\eta_c\,\mathrm{LHV})=1400(14.7)/44{,}000=0.47\). So the cycle in that problem corresponds to a lean mixture, which is typical of CI engines (page 1.2.2).

Class Activity — Pair-solving on air-fuel ratio

Given engine speed, \(e_v\), displacement and a fuel flow reading, compute \(\dot m_a\), \((A/F)\), \(\phi\) and the heat input per kg of air. Decide whether the setting suits SI economy, SI power or a catalyst.
