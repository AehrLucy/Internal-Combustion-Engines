2.4.2 Excess Air and Product Analysis

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.4 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Balance combustion with excess or deficient air and convert between wet and dry product analysis.

Lecture notes

Air suppliedConditionProducts contain
\(\lambda=1\)stoichiometricCO₂, H₂O, N₂ only
\(\lambda>1\)excess air (lean)leftover O₂, cooler flame
\(\lambda<1\)deficient air (rich)CO, H₂, unburned HC, soot

Relative air-fuel ratio

\[\lambda=\frac{(A/F)_{\mathrm{actual}}}{(A/F)_s}\]

Source: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3; Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4.

Excess-air fraction \(e\) and the other ways of stating air supply

\[e=\lambda-1=\frac{1}{\phi}-1,\qquad \lambda=1+e,\qquad \phi=\frac{1}{1+e}\]

\[\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_{\mathrm{actual}}=\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_s(1+e)=\lambda\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_s\]

Many course notes and board-review texts write \((A/F)_{actual}=(A/F)_{theo}(1+e)\) with \(e\) as a fraction (0.25 means 25% excess air). Heywood and Pulkrabek use \(\lambda\) and \(\phi\). They carry the same information.

Source: Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3 (definitions of \(\phi\) and \(\lambda\)); Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters) (excess air).

Exhaust analyzers such as the Orsat apparatus report dry volume percentages because the water is condensed out. To compare with a calculated composition, remove the H₂O from the product count first. When measured O₂ and N₂ are known, the excess air can be found from them.

Excess air from dry flue-gas analysis (no CO present)

\[\mathrm{excess\ air}=\frac{y_{\mathrm{O_2}}}{0.264\,y_{\mathrm{N_2}}-y_{\mathrm{O_2}}}\]

\(y_{O_2}\) and \(y_{N_2}\) are the dry volume fractions measured in the flue gas.

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters) (excess air and Orsat analysis).

Worked Example 6 — Methane with 20% excess air

CH₄ burns with 20% excess air. Find the dry product composition and the air-fuel ratio.

\[\mathrm{CH_4}+2.4\left(\mathrm{O_2}+3.76\,\mathrm{N_2}\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{CO_2}+2\,\mathrm{H_2O}+0.4\,\mathrm{O_2}+9.02\,\mathrm{N_2}\]

\[n_{\mathrm{dry}}=1+0.4+9.02=10.42\ \mathrm{kmol}\]

\[y_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=\frac{1}{10.42}=9.6\%,\quad y_{\mathrm{O_2}}=\frac{0.4}{10.42}=3.8\%\]

\[\left(\frac{A}{F}\right)_s=\frac{2(4.76)(28.97)}{16.04}=17.19,\quad\frac{A}{F}=1.2\times17.19=20.6\]

Answer: Dry: CO₂ 9.6%, O₂ 3.8%, N₂ 86.6%; \(A/F=20.6\).

Check with the excess-air formula: \(0.038/(0.264\times0.866-0.038)=0.199\approx20\%\).

Method from: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters); Heywood, Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals (2nd ed.), Ch. 3.

Class Activity — Group problem-solving on exhaust gas analysis

Each group is given a different Orsat reading (for example 10.5% CO₂, 5% O₂, 84.5% N₂ dry) for a fuel of known composition. Find the excess air and the air-fuel ratio, then check by writing the balanced reaction.

Check Your Understanding

  1. Why must H₂O be removed before comparing with an Orsat reading?
  2. Write the products for propane burned at \(\lambda=1.3\).
  3. What happens to the CO₂ percentage in the dry products as excess air increases?
