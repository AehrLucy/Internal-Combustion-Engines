2.4.1 Gas Mixture Relations

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.4 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Use mole, volume and mass fractions of ideal-gas mixtures to find molar mass, partial pressure and mixture properties.

Lecture notes

Air, fuel gases and combustion products are all treated as ideal-gas mixtures. Dalton’s law states that each component exerts its partial pressure as if it alone occupied the volume, and Amagat’s law states that each occupies a partial volume at the mixture pressure. For ideal gases, volume fraction equals mole fraction.

Mole fraction and partial pressure

\[y_i=\frac{n_i}{n},\qquad p_i=y_i\,p\]

\(n_i\) is the kmol of component \(i\) and \(n\) the total kmol.

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters) (Dalton and Amagat laws); Heywood, Ch. 4.

Mixture molar mass and mass fraction

\[M=\sum_i y_iM_i,\qquad x_i=\frac{y_iM_i}{M}\]

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters); Heywood, Ch. 4.

Mixture specific heat (mass basis)

\[c_{p,\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_i x_i\,c_{p,i}\]

Source: Faires, Thermodynamics (combustion and gas-mixture chapters); Heywood, Ch. 4.

For example, air is \(y_{O_2}=0.21\) and \(y_{N_2}=0.79\), giving \(M=0.21(32)+0.79(28)=28.97\) kg/kmol after rounding.

Note!

The Unit I formula sheet uses \(PV=mRT\) with \(R=0.287\) kJ/kg·K for air. For a mixture, use \(R=R_u/M\) with the mixture molar mass found here (\(R_u=8.314\) kJ/kmol·K).

Check Your Understanding

  1. A gas is 60% CH₄ and 40% CO₂ by volume. Find its molar mass and the mass fraction of CH₄.
  2. Why is volume fraction equal to mole fraction only for ideal gases?
