2.3.1 Processing and Manufacturing Routes

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.3 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Describe the main routes by which raw resources become engine and burner fuels, and say which fuel property each process changes.

Lecture notes

Fuels seldom come ready to use. Processing adjusts the properties you studied in 2.2: heating value, octane or cetane number, volatility, sulfur content and physical form.

RouteWhat happensProperty it changes
Distillationcrude oil is separated by boiling range into gases, naphtha (gasoline), kerosene, diesel, residuesets the volatility range of each product
Cracking (thermal, catalytic, hydro-)large molecules are broken into gasoline-range moleculesraises gasoline yield
Reforming and alkylationmolecules are rearranged into branched and aromatic hydrocarbonsraises octane number
Hydrotreatinghydrogen removes sulfur and nitrogen compoundslowers sulfur and emissions
Drying, pulverizing, briquetting, carbonizationsolid fuel is dried, ground, compacted or heated without air to make coke or charcoalraises heating value per kg, improves burning in pulverized-coal burners
Gasificationpartial oxidation of a solid with air or steam gives producer gas (CO, H₂, CO₂, N₂)converts a solid to a gas an engine can burn; LHV about 4–6 MJ/m³ with air blast
Fermentation, transesterification, anaerobic digestionsugars become ethanol; vegetable oils become biodiesel; wastes become biogasmakes renewable liquid and gaseous fuels
Gas sweetening and dehydrationH₂S and CO₂ and water are removed from natural gas; LPG is recoveredimproves heating value and corrosion behavior

Note!

Processing explains the fuel numbers used in Unit I. Raising the octane number lets an SI engine use a higher compression ratio, and the Otto efficiency formula then shows the efficiency gain. Lower-sulfur diesel is what makes CI exhaust aftertreatment (page 1.2.2, NOₓ and particulate row) workable.
