2.3.2 Processing Metrics

ME 3115 • CO2 • Lesson 2.3 • LO 2.1

Learning focus: Compute fuel processing metrics such as cold-gas efficiency, yield and removal fraction.

Lecture notes

A processing plant is judged by how much of the input energy or mass ends up in the useful product. Three metrics are used most often.

  • Yield = mass of product / mass of feed.
  • Removal fraction (for example sulfur) = (inlet − outlet) / inlet.
  • Cold-gas efficiency of a gasifier = chemical energy in the product gas / chemical energy in the solid fuel fed.

Cold-gas efficiency of a gasifier

\[\eta_{cg}=\frac{\dot{V}_g\,\mathrm{LHV}_g}{\dot{m}_s\,\mathrm{LHV}_s}\]

\(\dot V_g\) is the volumetric flow of product gas, \(\mathrm{LHV}_g\) its volumetric LHV, \(\dot m_s\) the solid fuel feed rate and \(\mathrm{LHV}_s\) its LHV. “Cold” means sensible heat in the hot gas is not counted.

Source: Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4 and Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters (producer gas and alternative fuels); Maleev, Internal Combustion Engines: Theory and Design (fuels and combustion chapters).

Worked Example 5 — Gasifier cold-gas efficiency

Wood is fed at 100 kg/h with LHV 15 MJ/kg. Producer gas is made at 180 m³/h with LHV 5.0 MJ/m³.

\[\dot{E}_{\mathrm{wood}}=100\times15=1500\ \mathrm{MJ/h}\]

\[\dot{E}_{\mathrm{gas}}=180\times5.0=900\ \mathrm{MJ/h}\]

\[\eta_{cg}=\frac{900}{1500}=0.60\]

Answer: \(\eta_{cg}=60\%\).

The other 40% leaves as sensible heat, tar, char and losses.

Method from: Pulkrabek, Engineering Fundamentals of the Internal Combustion Engine, Ch. 4; Stone, Introduction to Internal Combustion Engines (4th ed.), thermochemistry and fuels chapters.

Note!

Producer gas has a low LHV per m³, so a port-fueled engine on producer gas loses power because less fuel energy enters per cycle. This links to volumetric efficiency \(e_v\) and brake mean effective pressure from page 1.3.1.
